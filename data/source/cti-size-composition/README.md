# Household-size composition candidate inputs

The candidate script `scripts/plan39/size-composition-candidate.mjs` reads two normalized extracts from primary sources. The Household Survey panel is already extracted from the 26 original annual workbooks under `raw/` by `scripts/plan39/prepare-size-composition-panel.mjs`.

- `expenditure.csv`: columns `year,size,category,value_yen`; 2005–2017; bins `1,2,3,4,5+`; categories `消費支出` and all 10 major categories. Use detailed annual result table 3-1 for two-or-more-person households and table 1 for single households. Values are nominal yen per household per month. The 5+ cell is weighted by the same-year table 3-1 counts for 5-person and 6+ households, not by an unweighted mean.
- `weights.csv`: columns `year,size,households,series_id,benchmark_id,connection_status,interpolationMethod,interpolationEndpoints,vintageCaveat`; 2005–2017 and 2025; bins `1,2,3,4,5+`. The published 2005–2010 and 2012–2017 figures are legacy annual Labour Force Survey IV-4 values and are not rebased onto one connected population benchmark. The 2011 row is a diagnostic interpolation: average the 2010 and 2012 shares by size, renormalize rounded shares, then scale by the average endpoint total count. 2010 IV-4 (`statInfId=000008597994`) uses the 2005 Census base; 2012 IV-4 (`statInfId=000018854323`) uses the 2010 Census base. No common-base bridge is established. 2011 IV-4 (`statInfId=000012675623`) excludes Iwate, Miyagi, and Fukushima, so it is not used as a national household-size weight. Interpolated rows are marked `linear_share_interpolation`, identify endpoints, and retain this vintage caveat. The lambda fit excludes 2011, while annual R includes it; endpoint alternatives are reported as sensitivity diagnostics. The 2025 reference counts are from annual IV-4 (`statInfId=000040403950`). All weights remain explicitly tagged `unverified_connected_series` and produce only the separate `legacy_unharmonized_weights` diagnostic. Do not interpret them as the official CTI weighting series.

Source references:

- Two-or-more-household annual table 004, “用途分類（世帯人員別）”, e-Stat table ID `0002070009`: https://www.e-stat.go.jp/stat-search/database?kana=46&layout=dataset&statdisp_id=0002070009
- Detailed annual results table 3-1 “世帯人員別” (the year-specific Excel source files and IDs are recorded in `household-survey-panel-manifest.json`): https://www.e-stat.go.jp/stat-search/files?cycle=7&layout=datalist&month=0&tclass1=000000330001&tclass2=000000330004&tclass3=000000330005&tclass4val=0&toukei=00200561&tstat=000000330001&year=YYYY0
- Single-household annual detailed results table 1, e-Stat year listing: https://www.e-stat.go.jp/stat-search/files?cycle=7&layout=datalist&month=0&tclass1=000000330001&tclass2=000000330022&tclass3=000000330023&tclass4val=0&toukei=00200561&tstat=000000330001&year=YYYY0
- Labour Force Survey annual IV-4 household table for 2025 (2025 reference counts): https://www.e-stat.go.jp/stat-search/file-download?fileKind=0&statInfId=000040403950
- 2025-based CTI methodology (the fixed-reference household distributions): https://www.stat.go.jp/data/cti/pdf/micro_ref_2025.pdf

## Method and current status

The 2005–2017 expenditure panel is present and reproducible from the saved official files. The two-or-more-household table 3-1 contains annual category spending and that year's 2/3/4/5/6+ household distribution. The distribution in `two-plus-household-distribution.csv` is conditional on two-or-more-person households, so it is not used as an all-household size weight.

The older `size-composition-candidate.mjs` applies 2025 all-household Labour Force Survey proportions as a fixed reference, then normalizes each year to 2017. Because earlier IV-4 counts remain on unverified benchmark vintages and 2011 is interpolated across a benchmark break, that separate panel/lambda candidate remains an analysis-only sensitivity diagnostic; it does not claim an officially connected 2005–2017 series and is not the provisional production correction documented below. The 2011 annual R uses interpolated shares, but lambda fitting excludes 2011. The candidate JSON/CSV report alternatives that use the 2010 or 2012 endpoint share for 2011. The 2017 A/B ratio is only a level anchor; its identity at the anchor is not treated as model fit or backtest evidence.

## Provisional production endpoint correction

The separate `production-pi2plus.json` input is generated by
`node scripts/plan39/build-production-pi2plus.mjs`. Its manifest records and
hashes `weights.csv`, `weights-source-manifest.json`, the January
`lfs-national-shares.csv`, its source trace, all retained January source files,
and the generated artifact. The production loader verifies those hashes before
using the data; it does not load the research backtest output directly.

For each of the nine CTI categories and Other, production first computes the
existing 2017 A/B anchor estimate. It estimates a category-specific slope from
the 2025 adjusted-minus-baseline endpoint residual divided by the 2017-to-2025
January `setai-n` 2+ share change. It applies that slope to the change in
historical IV-4 2+ share from its own 2017 value. The historical IV-4 and
January `setai-n` shares use different source/vintage definitions; centering
historical pi at 2017 makes the correction zero at the anchor but does not
validate a bridge between the distributions. The nine corrected major
categories plus corrected Other define the total, and the result records a
floating-point reconciliation check.

The runtime calibrates and validates against the saved nominal annual B/A JSON
artifacts, whose index values are rounded to one decimal. It does not use the
higher-precision raw-workbook values from the separate research backtests.
Accordingly, the production-precision 2018–2024 MAE/RMSE below may differ from
those research-only figures.

This is a provisional predictive correction, not a causal household-size
effect. Its 2018–2024 comparison is retrospective because 2025 is used to
calibrate the endpoint slope. The 2011 IV-4 share is included as the existing
synthetic interpolation from unverified 2010/2012 endpoints, as a provisional
completion; it is not an observed national value and should be revised if a
verified national value becomes available. The January shares are proxies for
the trailing 12-month adjustment distribution. Survey redesign and population
benchmark changes remain caveats. Regenerate the pi artifact after changing
any upstream source and rerun `pnpm plan39:analysis` to produce a matching
fingerprint.

For the current nominal inputs, 2018–2024 baseline MAE/RMSE are `1.8356858` /
`2.1818629` index points; endpoint-corrected values are `0.0775891` /
`0.0848610`. These production-precision values are in
`results/plan39/plan39-analysis-a8c7ad0fa68a5208.json`. The exact official
maximum Other-share jump is `0.7639419` percentage points; the effective
threshold rounds this upward to `0.77`. The corrected 2016→2017 jump of
`0.7661765` points passes, and the refreshed V2 publication gate is accepted.
This gate result does not make the retrospective validation independent or
establish a causal household-size effect.

### 2017–2018 education stability diagnostic

The separate stability analysis uses source-year household-size profiles and `setai-n.xls` LFS special-tabulation shares published as the adjustment input for each year's January Household Survey results. Household Survey sample-design documentation defines the adjustment distribution as the LFS household-size distribution averaged over the latest 12 months; it is not confirmed as a calendar-year average. This is not annual IV-4 and not the 2025 CTI joint size-by-age reference distribution, and it does not replace existing IV-4 candidate inputs. See [Household Survey 2018 report, p.25](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/pdf/18gai00.pdf) and [Household size distribution](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/setai_bunpu.html). The analysis records the 2018 Household Survey table 3-1 (two-or-more households) and table 1 (single households), using the same five size bins (`1,2,3,4,5+`) for both years. The Household Survey comprehensively revised its ledger format in 2018, so the cross-year profile comparison is not a seamless time series.

The diagnostic reads the 2025-based official CTI annual workbooks directly: basic B `statInfId=000040499069` and adjusted A `statInfId=000040499087`, sheet `総・年`, nominal columns J:T. It traces row 10/11 (A, 2017/2018) and row 25/26 (B, 2017/2018), with header row 9 and category mapping in the result JSON and source manifest. The published total and nine categories are numeric; the T column for Other is `-`, so Other is derived as total minus the nine categories and its A/B value is not represented as an official published Other ratio. Direct XLSX extraction avoids the rounded one-decimal A/B JSON artifacts, which make the 2017 education A/B ratio falsely appear exactly 1. The 2017 ratios are level anchors only. The 2018 A/B rows are held out for comparison in the historical-lambda prediction; a separately reported two-year lambda/geometric-anchor scenario uses 2018 in-sample and is not validation. Its denominator `log(Q2018,total/Q2017,total)` is retained and flagged as unstable when small.

Recreate the supplemental diagnostic with `node scripts/plan39/education-stability-2017-2018.mjs`. It writes the raw-source trace manifest, normalized `profiles.csv` and `setai-special-shares.csv`, and `education-stability-2017-2018.json/.csv` under the candidate analysis paths. No production estimator, loader, publication gate, or quarterly value consumes this analysis.

### 2017–2025 2+ household-share backtest

`scripts/plan39/two-plus-share-backtest.mjs` is a separate research-only comparison of the proposed annual education-share formula against the 2017-share anchor baseline. It reads exact raw values from the retained official 2025-base CTI XLSX files and the official annual January `setai-n` files for 2017–2025. The January source URL, SHA-256, sheet, reported year, national column, denominator, exact 1/2/3/4/5+ counts and shares, and row/cell extraction evidence are recorded in `two-plus-share-backtest/source-trace.json`; the extracted national shares are also written to `two-plus-share-backtest/lfs-national-shares.csv`. Raw official files are retained in `raw/lfs-special-jan/`.

The calibration is only 2017: `K=(pAedu_2017/pBedu_2017)/pi2plus_2017`, where `pAedu=A_education/A_total`, `pBedu=B_education/B_total`, and `pi2plus=1-pi_1`. The candidate is `pHat_Aedu_t=pBedu_t*pi2plus_t*K`; the comparison baseline is `pHat_Aedu_t=pBedu_t*(pAedu_2017/pBedu_2017)`. 2017 is excluded from validation. Per-year predictions/residuals and 2018–2025 coverage, MAE, RMSE, and bias are in `results/plan39/two-plus-share-backtest/annual-backtest.csv` and `summary.json`. Across 8/8 validation years, the anchor baseline has MAE `0.06714`, RMSE `0.08422`, and bias `+0.06169` percentage points; the `pi2plus` model has MAE `0.11490`, RMSE `0.14372`, and bias `−0.09042` percentage points. Thus adding `pi2plus` worsens both absolute-error metrics in this backtest. The 2017–2025 source trace includes unrounded raw CTI totals/education values and their XLSX cell locations. If a validation year fails its source checks, that year is omitted and coverage/failure details are recorded; a missing or invalid 2017 calibration input stops the backtest without a new result.

The `setai-n` distribution is the special tabulation published for each year's January Household Survey. The Household Survey design describes the correction distribution for a month as an LFS household-size distribution averaged over the preceding 12 months, so a January snapshot is only an annual proxy and is not a calendar-year average. LFS population benchmarks changed to the 2015 Census base beginning in January 2017 and to the 2020 Census base beginning in January 2022; the 2021/2022 movement may combine actual composition change with rebenchmarking. The 2018 Household Survey redesign is another comparability caveat for the adjusted CTI target. References: [Household size distribution](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/setai_bunpu.html), [2018 Household Survey report, p.25](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kakei/pdf/18gai00.pdf), [2017 benchmark revision](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/roudou/benchpop/change/2017.html), and [2022 benchmark revision](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/roudou/benchpop/change.html).

Recreate with `node scripts/plan39/two-plus-share-backtest.mjs`. This diagnostic does not feed production estimators, loaders, publication gates, quarterly values, or the previous 2017/2018 education stability artifacts.

### 2017-anchor-only category-share backtest

`scripts/plan39/anchor-only-category-backtest.mjs` uses exact numeric cells from the retained 2025-base CTI basic B and adjusted A workbooks (`総・年`) for all ten expenditure classifications. Exact Japanese header matches map the nine published lower categories; `Other` uses its numeric workbook value in 2020–2025 and is derived as total minus those nine categories in 2017–2019, where the workbook cell is `-`. The source status and cell coordinates are captured by workbook/year. Direct Other values differ from the residual by at most 0.0004 index points in B (2020) and 0.0003 in A (2021), so the 2020 switch from residual to official Other is a category-specific comparability caveat.

The per-category anchor is `q[c]=pA[2017,c]/pB[2017,c]`; the requested raw prediction is `pHatRaw[t,c]=pB[t,c]*q[c]`. The separately labeled coherent variant divides each raw prediction by that year's sum across categories. Both are validated on 2018–2025 only (8 years); category errors and MAE/RMSE/bias are reported in percentage points. `results/plan39/anchor-only-category-backtest/summary.json` records formulas, metrics, annual values, source hashes and exact cells; `annual-category-backtest.csv` contains the 80 validation category-year rows and each year's raw predicted category-share sum. Recreate with `node scripts/plan39/anchor-only-category-backtest.mjs`. This is research-only and is not consumed by production estimators, loaders, gates, or quarterly data.

Run the extractor and candidate calculator with:

node scripts/plan39/prepare-size-composition-panel.mjs
